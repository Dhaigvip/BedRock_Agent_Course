TOKYO TRAVEL GUIDE
==================

Overview
--------
Tokyo is Japan's capital and the world's most populous metropolitan area. It blends
ultra-modern skyscrapers with historic temples, world-class cuisine, and one of the
safest urban environments on the planet.

Best Time to Visit
------------------
Spring (March–May): Cherry blossom season. Temperatures 10–18°C. Book accommodation
6+ months in advance — this is peak season.
Autumn (September–November): Cooler weather, red maple leaves, fewer crowds than spring.
Avoid: July–August (hot, humid, typhoon season) and Golden Week (late April to early May)
when domestic travel surges and prices spike.

Visa Requirements
-----------------
Citizens of 68 countries including USA, UK, Canada, Australia, and most of Europe can
enter Japan visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism. Passport must be valid for the
duration of stay. An onward ticket is recommended.
Stays longer than 90 days or for purposes other than tourism require a visa applied
for in advance at a Japanese embassy.

Getting Around
--------------
IC Card (Suica / Pasmo): Load a rechargeable transit card at any station. Works on
all JR trains, Tokyo Metro, buses, and many convenience stores and vending machines.
Cost: 500 yen deposit + top-up amount.
Day passes: Tokyo Metro 24/48/72-hour passes available at airports and major stations.
Taxis: Expensive. Base fare ~730 yen. Avoid during rush hour.
Walking: Central areas (Shinjuku, Shibuya, Asakusa) are very walkable.

Top Neighbourhoods
------------------
Shinjuku: Shopping, nightlife, Kabukicho entertainment district, Golden Gai bar alley.
Shibuya: Famous scramble crossing, youth fashion, Harajuku nearby.
Asakusa: Traditional Tokyo, Senso-ji temple, Nakamise shopping street.
Akihabara: Electronics, anime, manga culture.
Ginza: Luxury shopping, galleries, high-end dining.
Yanaka: Old Tokyo feel, independent shops, quiet temples.

Must-See Attractions
--------------------
Senso-ji Temple (Asakusa): Tokyo's oldest temple, free entry, best visited early morning.
Meiji Jingu Shrine (Harajuku): Forested Shinto shrine, free entry.
teamLab Borderless / Planets: Immersive digital art. Book tickets well in advance online.
Tsukiji Outer Market: Fresh seafood, sushi breakfast. Arrive by 7am.
Shibuya Crossing: Best viewed from Starbucks or Mag's Park observation deck, free.
Tokyo Skytree: 634m tower. Entry ~3,000 yen. Book online to skip queues.
Shinjuku Gyoen: Large garden, beautiful in cherry blossom and autumn. Entry 500 yen.

Food Guide
----------
Ramen: Budget ~800–1,200 yen. Try Ichiran (solo booths) or Fuunji (tsukemen style).
Sushi: Conveyor belt (kaiten-zushi) ~1,500–3,000 yen. Standing sushi bars ~2,000 yen.
Counter omakase: 10,000–30,000+ yen. Reserve weeks in advance.
Izakaya (pub dining): 3,000–5,000 yen per person with drinks.
Convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson): Surprisingly good onigiri, sandwiches,
hot foods. Budget meals under 500 yen.
Depachika (department store basement food halls): Premium bento, pastries, prepared foods.

Budget Breakdown (per day)
---------------------------
Budget traveller: 5,000–8,000 yen (~$35–55 USD)
  Hostel dorm: 2,500–4,000 yen
  Convenience store meals x3: 1,500 yen
  Transport: 600–1,000 yen

Mid-range: 15,000–25,000 yen (~$100–170 USD)
  Business hotel: 8,000–15,000 yen
  Ramen/sushi lunch, izakaya dinner: 4,000–6,000 yen
  Transport + 1 paid attraction: 2,000–3,000 yen

Luxury: 50,000+ yen (~$335+ USD)
  Boutique or luxury hotel: 30,000–80,000 yen
  Omakase dinner: 15,000–30,000 yen
  Private transport + guided experiences

Practical Tips
--------------
Cash: Japan remains largely cash-based. Always carry yen. 7-Eleven ATMs accept
foreign cards reliably 24/7.
Wi-Fi / SIM: Pocket Wi-Fi rental or eSIM available at airports. Strongly recommended.
Etiquette: Remove shoes when entering homes and many traditional restaurants.
Do not eat or drink while walking. Speak quietly on public transport.
Tipping: NOT customary in Japan. Good service is standard and expected — tipping can
cause confusion or offence.
Power: 100V, Type A plugs (same as USA). European visitors need an adapter.
Emergency: Police 110, Ambulance/Fire 119.
