BANGKOK TRAVEL GUIDE
====================

Overview
--------
Bangkok (officially Krung Thep Maha Nakhon) is Thailand's capital and largest city.
It is a city of contrasts: ornate temples beside neon-lit street markets, luxury malls
next to floating markets, and world-famous street food at every corner. It is one of
the world's most visited cities and an excellent base for exploring Thailand.

Best Time to Visit
------------------
Cool season (November–February): Best weather. 25–32°C, low humidity, little rain.
Peak tourist season — book accommodation in advance.
Hot season (March–May): Very hot (35–40°C) and humid. Songkran water festival in April.
Wet/monsoon season (June–October): Daily afternoon showers, lower prices, fewer tourists.
Avoid: Major flooding can occur September–October in some years.

Visa Requirements
-----------------
Visa exemption: Citizens of 93 countries including USA, UK, most of Europe, Australia,
Japan, and Singapore can enter Thailand visa-free for 60 days (as of 2024).
This was recently extended from 30 days — verify current rules before travel.
Tourist visa (TR): Available for 60 days, extendable by 30 days at an immigration office.
Thailand Elite visa: Long-term option (5–20 years) for frequent visitors.
No visa on arrival is needed for most Western passport holders.

Getting Around
--------------
BTS Skytrain: Elevated rail covering Sukhumvit and Silom corridors. ~20–60 baht per trip.
MRT: Underground metro, connects to BTS at interchange stations.
BRT: Bus rapid transit along Narathiwat road.
Rabbit Card / MRT Card: Rechargeable transit cards for BTS/MRT, accept contactless.
Chao Phraya Express Boat: River ferry connecting riverside temples and the old city.
Tuk-tuks: Iconic but negotiate the fare before boarding. Tourist traps common.
Motorbike taxis: Orange-vested riders at most BTS exits. Fast for short trips.
Grab (SE Asia's Uber): Reliable, metered fare shown in advance. Strongly recommended.
Taxis: Use the meter, say "Meter, please" (or show the Thai on your phone). Never accept
a flat rate from a taxi at tourist sites.

Top Neighbourhoods
------------------
Sukhumvit: Expat hub, international restaurants, nightlife, BTS-connected.
Silom/Sathon: Business district by day, Patpong night market, rooftop bars.
Rattanakosin (Old City): Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Wat Arun, riverside.
Banglamphu/Khao San Road: Budget backpacker area, cheap guesthouses and bars.
Ari: Trendy neighbourhood with independent cafés, brunch spots, local vibe.
Chatuchak: Home to the world's largest weekend market (JJ Market, 15,000+ stalls).

Must-See Attractions
--------------------
Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew: Thailand's most sacred site. Entry 500 baht. Dress modestly
(shoulders and knees covered). Arrive early to beat crowds and heat.
Wat Pho: Temple of the Reclining Buddha. Entry 200 baht. Traditional massage school on-site.
Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn): Best seen at sunset from across the river. Entry 100 baht.
Chatuchak Weekend Market: Saturday–Sunday. Arrive before 11am before the heat peaks.
Floating markets: Damnoen Saduak (90 min away) or Amphawa (weekend, closer, more authentic).
Lumphini Park: 57-hectare green space. Free. Excellent for morning exercise with locals.
Jim Thompson House: Silk merchant's preserved Thai house. Guided tours, entry 200 baht.

Food Guide
----------
Street food: 40–80 baht per dish. Pad thai, som tum (papaya salad), khao man gai,
mango sticky rice, boat noodles. Yaowarat (Chinatown) for night food.
Local restaurants: 80–200 baht per dish.
Malls / food courts: 100–200 baht for a full meal. Air-conditioned. Terminal 21, MBK.
Mid-range restaurant: 300–800 baht per person.
Fine dining (rooftop or chef's table): 1,500–5,000+ baht per person.
Caution: Avoid ice from street vendors if you have a sensitive stomach. Bottled water ~10 baht.

Budget Breakdown (per day)
---------------------------
Budget traveller: 800–1,500 baht (~$22–42 USD)
  Hostel dorm or guesthouse: 300–600 baht
  Street food x3 meals: 200–300 baht
  BTS/MRT/Grab: 150–300 baht

Mid-range: 2,500–5,000 baht (~$70–140 USD)
  3-star hotel: 1,200–2,500 baht
  Local restaurants + 1 nicer dinner: 600–1,000 baht
  Transport + 1–2 attractions: 500–800 baht

Luxury: 8,000–20,000+ baht (~$220–560+ USD)
  Luxury hotel (Mandarin Oriental, Peninsula, etc.): 6,000–15,000 baht
  Rooftop dinner + cocktails: 2,000–5,000 baht
  Private driver for the day: 2,000–3,000 baht

Practical Tips
--------------
Heat: Hydrate constantly. Carry a small water bottle. Seek air-conditioned breaks.
Temple dress code: Shoulders and knees must be covered at all temples. Sarongs often
available to borrow or rent at major sites.
Scams: Avoid strangers who approach you near tourist sites offering "special tours" or
"closed today" stories — the Grand Palace is never closed to tourists.
Currency: Thai Baht (THB). ATMs widely available. Foreign ATMs charge ~200 baht fee.
Superrich exchange booths (orange or green) offer better rates than airports or hotels.
Health: Hepatitis A vaccine recommended. Dengue fever risk — use mosquito repellent.
Emergency: Police 191, Ambulance 1669, Tourist Police (English) 1155.
