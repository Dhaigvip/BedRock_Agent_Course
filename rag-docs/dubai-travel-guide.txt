DUBAI TRAVEL GUIDE
==================

Overview
--------
Dubai is the largest city in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and a global hub for
business, luxury tourism, and architecture. Known for record-breaking skyscrapers,
the world's largest mall, indoor ski slopes, and a tax-free shopping environment,
it has transformed from a desert trading post into a modern metropolis in under 50 years.

Best Time to Visit
------------------
Winter (November–March): Ideal. Temperatures 18–28°C, low humidity, outdoor activities
possible. Peak tourist season with highest hotel prices.
Spring (April–May): Getting hot but tolerable. Shoulder season, better prices.
Summer (June–September): Extreme heat (40–48°C) and very high humidity. Outdoor
activities impractical. Significant discounts on hotels and flights.
Ramadan: Dates vary annually. Eating, drinking, and smoking in public during daylight
hours is prohibited. Many restaurants close during the day. Respectful behaviour required.

Visa Requirements
-----------------
Visa on arrival / visa-free: Citizens of 50+ countries including USA, UK, EU, Australia,
Canada, Japan, and South Korea receive a free 30-day visa on arrival, extendable once.
Some nationalities can get 90 days visa-free.
UAE tourist e-visa: Available online for other nationalities before travel.
Israeli passport holders: Can visit the UAE following the 2020 Abraham Accords.
Check the UAE government portal for the latest list as rules change frequently.
Note: Entry can be refused at UAE immigration officer discretion.

Getting Around
--------------
Dubai Metro: Two lines (Red and Green). Covers airport, Downtown, Marina, Deira.
Nol Card: Rechargeable transit card, works on metro, buses, trams, and water buses.
Single metro trip: ~3–7 AED depending on zones.
Dubai Tram: Runs along JBR/Marina waterfront, connects to metro.
RTA buses: Extensive but slow. Air-conditioned stops throughout the city.
Taxis: Plentiful, metered, air-conditioned. Starting fare 12 AED. Safe and reliable.
Careem / Uber: Both operate in Dubai. Usually similar price to metered taxis.
Car rental: Popular for visiting areas not well served by metro (Jumeirah, Miracle Garden).

Top Neighbourhoods
------------------
Downtown Dubai: Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall, Dubai Fountain. Iconic and tourist-heavy.
Dubai Marina: Waterfront promenade, luxury yachts, restaurants, The Beach at JBR.
Jumeirah Beach Residence (JBR): Outdoor mall, beach, dining strip.
Old Dubai / Deira: Gold Souk, Spice Souk, traditional abra (water taxi) rides.
Al Quoz: Art galleries, Design District (d3), creative hub.
Palm Jumeirah: Artificial island, Atlantis resort, luxury hotels, private beaches.

Must-See Attractions
--------------------
Burj Khalifa: World's tallest building (828m). Level 124/125 ticket from ~130 AED.
Book online in advance — "At the Top" tickets often sell out. Level 148 premium available.
Dubai Mall: World's largest mall by total area. Includes Dubai Aquarium (~130 AED),
Dubai Ice Rink, and the Dubai Fountain (free show every 30 min after 6pm).
Burj Al Arab: Iconic sail-shaped hotel. Not open to non-guests unless for dining.
Desert Safari: Half-day or full-day dune bashing + camel ride + Bedouin camp dinner.
~250–400 AED per person from reputable operators.
Gold Souk (Deira): 300+ shops selling gold jewellery. Fixed-weight gold prices,
negotiate making charges. Most trustworthy gold market in the world.
Miracle Garden: 150+ million flowers (October–April only). ~55 AED entry.

Food Guide
----------
Dubai has an exceptionally diverse food scene driven by its expat population
(over 85% of residents are foreign nationals).
Street food / cheap eats: Shawarma 5–15 AED, falafel wrap 5–10 AED, biryani 15–25 AED.
Mid-range restaurant: 60–150 AED per person.
Mall food courts: 30–60 AED for a meal.
Brunch (Friday): Dubai's famous institution. Hotels offer all-inclusive brunches
with alcohol from ~350–700 AED per person. Popular at Jumeirah, Le Méridien, etc.
Fine dining: 300–800+ AED per person. Dubai has 3 Michelin-starred restaurants.
Alcohol: Only available in licensed venues (hotels, certain clubs). Not sold in
supermarkets. Dry areas exist in older parts of the city.

Budget Breakdown (per day)
---------------------------
Budget traveller: 200–400 AED (~$55–110 USD)
  Budget hotel or hostel: 100–200 AED
  Shawarmas + supermarket: 40–60 AED
  Metro/bus: 20–30 AED

Mid-range: 600–1,200 AED (~$165–330 USD)
  3-4 star hotel: 350–700 AED
  Restaurant meals x2: 150–250 AED
  Taxis + 1 attraction: 100–200 AED

Luxury: 2,000–8,000+ AED (~$550–2,200+ USD)
  5-star hotel: 1,000–4,000 AED
  Fine dining dinner: 400–1,000 AED
  Private driver, desert safari, premium attractions

Practical Tips
--------------
Dress code: Conservative dress required outside tourist zones. Shoulders and knees
covered in malls, souks, and public spaces. Beachwear only at beaches and pool areas.
Photography: Do not photograph government buildings, military, airports, or people
without consent. Police and immigration facilities strictly off-limits.
Public displays of affection: Holding hands is acceptable. Kissing in public is illegal
and has led to tourist arrests.
Currency: UAE Dirham (AED). Pegged to USD at 3.67 AED = 1 USD. ATMs everywhere.
Credit cards accepted almost universally.
Friday: The weekend begins. Many businesses open late or run reduced hours Friday morning.
Weekend is Friday–Saturday; Sunday is a working day.
Emergency: Police 999, Ambulance 998, Fire 997, Tourist Police 800-4673.
