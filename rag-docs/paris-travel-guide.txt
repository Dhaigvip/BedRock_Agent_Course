PARIS TRAVEL GUIDE
==================

Overview
--------
Paris, the capital of France, is one of the world's great cultural centres. Famous for
the Eiffel Tower, world-class museums, haute cuisine, and fashion, it attracts over
30 million visitors per year. The city is divided into 20 arrondissements (districts)
arranged in a clockwise spiral from the centre.

Best Time to Visit
------------------
Spring (April–June): Pleasant 15–22°C, flowers blooming, fewer crowds than summer.
Autumn (September–October): Beautiful light, harvest season, locals back from vacation.
Avoid: July–August. Many Parisians leave the city, some restaurants close, tourist
crowds peak, and temperatures can reach 35°C+. School holidays also drive up prices.

Visa Requirements
-----------------
EU/EEA/Swiss citizens: No visa required, free movement.
USA, Canada, Australia, Japan, and most other Western countries: Visa-free for up to
90 days within any 180-day period under the Schengen Agreement.
ETIAS (EU Travel Information and Authorisation System): Expected to launch in 2025.
Travellers currently visa-exempt will need to apply online before travel. Cost ~€7.
Non-Schengen countries: Check requirements at the French embassy in your home country.

Getting Around
--------------
Metro: 16 lines covering the city. Ticket: ~€2.15 single, Carnet (10 tickets) ~€17.
Day/weekly passes (Navigo Easy or Navigo Semaine): Best value for multi-day visits.
RER: Suburban express trains connecting airports to city centre. CDG to Paris ~€11.
Vélib': City bike share. 24-hour pass ~€5. Excellent for flat areas like the Marais.
Walking: The best way to explore central Paris. Most major sights are walkable from each other.
Taxis / Uber: Available, metered taxis from ranks. Uber (called VTC) works throughout the city.

Top Neighbourhoods
------------------
Le Marais (3rd/4th): Historic Jewish quarter, trendy boutiques, art galleries, falafel.
Saint-Germain-des-Prés (6th): Literary cafés, antique dealers, upscale shopping.
Montmartre (18th): Sacré-Cœur, artists' quarter, village feel but very touristy.
Bastille/Oberkampf (11th): Local bars, restaurants, nightlife.
Canal Saint-Martin (10th): Hip cafés, independent shops, young Parisian crowd.
Île Saint-Louis: Tiny island, charming streets, famous Berthillon ice cream.

Must-See Attractions
--------------------
Eiffel Tower: Book tickets online well in advance. Summit ticket ~€29. Best photographed
from Trocadéro. Evening sparkle show runs for 5 minutes every hour after dark.
Louvre: World's largest art museum. Book online (~€17). Allow half a day minimum.
Musée d'Orsay: Impressionist masterpieces (Monet, Van Gogh). ~€16. Less crowded than Louvre.
Notre-Dame Cathedral: Undergoing restoration after 2019 fire; reopened December 2024.
Sainte-Chapelle: Gothic stained glass, often overlooked. ~€13, shorter queues.
Versailles: Day trip 40 min by RER C. Book well ahead. Palace + gardens ~€20+.
Père Lachaise Cemetery: Final resting place of Chopin, Oscar Wilde, Jim Morrison. Free.

Food Guide
----------
Boulangerie (bakery): Croissant ~€1.20, baguette ~€1.10. Essential every morning.
Café lunch: Formule (set menu) ~€14–18 for starter + main or main + dessert.
Bistro dinner: 3 courses ~€30–45 per person without wine.
Fine dining: €80–200+ per person. Book weeks or months ahead.
Marché (market): Marché d'Aligre and Marché Bastille for fresh produce and street food.
Wine: House carafe in a bistro from ~€5. Natural wine bars (cavistes) throughout the city.

Budget Breakdown (per day)
---------------------------
Budget traveller: €60–90
  Hostel dorm: €25–40
  Boulangerie breakfast, brasserie lunch formule, supermarket dinner: €20–30
  Metro day pass + 1 free museum (many free on first Sunday): €5–10

Mid-range: €150–250
  3-star hotel: €100–160
  Café breakfast, bistro lunch, bistro dinner: €50–70
  Metro + 1 paid museum: €20–30

Luxury: €400+
  Boutique or palace hotel: €250–800+
  Brasserie breakfast, gastronomic lunch or dinner: €100–200
  Private guided tours, chauffeured transport

Practical Tips
--------------
Language: A few words of French go a long way. Start with "Bonjour" and "Merci".
Pickpocketing: High-risk at Eiffel Tower, Sacré-Cœur, and on the Metro. Use a
money belt or inside pocket for valuables.
Museum passes: Paris Museum Pass (2/4/6 days from €52) covers 50+ museums,
skip ticket queues. Worth it for heavy museum days.
Tipping: Not mandatory. Round up or leave 5–10% for good service. Card payments
do not typically prompt for tips.
Water: Tap water is safe and free. Many public water fountains (Wallace fountains)
throughout the city.
Emergency: Police 17, SAMU (ambulance) 15, Fire 18. European emergency: 112.
